THE PADRE GARCIA POLYTECHNIC COLLEGE (PGPC): PURPOSE OF
ESTABLISHMENT, VISION STATEMENT, MISSION STATEMENT, CORE VALUES,
INSTITUTIONAL OBJECTIVES, AND QUALITY POLICY

Responding to the Call

Inspired by the aspirations and dreams of the youth from Padre Garcia through our
visionary leaders: former Congressman and Mayor Hon. Michael Angelo C. Rivera, Hon.
Mayor Celsa B. Rivera, and Hon. Vice Mayor Micko Angelo B. Rivera, they took a strong
leadership to establish the Padre Garcia Polytechnic College (PGPC). Their inner desire
to serve the disadvantaged sector of Padre Garcia was enshrined through the Municipal
Ordinance No. 42-2023 entitled: ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING THE PADRE GARCIA
POLYTECHNIC COLLEGE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES. PGPC will serve as the
strong arm of the local government of Padre Garcia province of Batangas and nearby
places in providing academic excellence and quality higher education.

Taking the Responsibility

The PADRE GARCIA POLYTECHNIC COLLEGE opened its door to more than nine
hundred students in 2024, of which 80% were Garciano residents. The admitted students
were grouped to morning and afternoon classes and enrolled to the first four-degree
courses duly approved by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) such as; BS
Criminology, BS Accounting Management, BS in Computer Science and Bachelor of
Public Administration. Dr. Nino J. Vinas was the first designated College President/ Vice
President for Academic Affairs since March 1, 2024, up to present. The concerted efforts
and dedication of the men and women behind PGPC operations were truly remarkable.

Preparing for the Challenges

The PGPC adheres its quality instruction, research and extension as manifested in the
college vision, mission, core values, educational objectives, long-term and medium-
term development plan, the higher education directions and trusts of the Commission
on Higher Education and the local government of Padre Garcia. The extra-curricular
activities through various student organizations and leadership training for the holistic
development of students shall be emphasized. Faculty and non-teaching personnel
development programs were put in place to empower teachers and support staff in the
area of instruction, research, and extension.

Adapting to the Winds of Change

The PADRE GARCIA POLYTECHNIC COLLEGE recognizes the importance of human
resources for sustainable organizational development. As such, it hired highly competent
teaching and non-teaching personnel to deliver the best instructions and services to our
stakeholders anchored on the desired goals and objectives of the College aligned with
our vision, mission, core values and quality policy. Notable men and women inspired
innovations and stirred development for the College.

Chapter III
STUDENT RETENTION

Section A -Classification of Students
Generally, the college has only one (1) type of student enrolled in the baccalaureate
programs
Degree program students (undergraduates) will fall under any of the following
student classification:
1. New freshmen - these are incoming first year students
2. Old Freshmen - these are students who have finished less than 25% of the total
number of units required under their 4-year degree program
3. Sophomore - these are students who have finished not less than 25% but no more
than 50% of the total number of units required under their 4-year degree program
4. Junior - these are students who have finished at least 50% but no more than 75%
of the total number of units required under their 4-year degree program
5. Senior - these are students who have finished at least 75% of the total number of
units required under their 4-year degree program.

Section B - Qualifying Exams
All incoming 3rd year students enrolled to board programs are required to take
and pass the qualifying exam (QE) as requirement for enrollment of the succeeding
semester. QE shall be done during the 2nd year, 2nd semester of every academic year.
If failed on the first attempt, the student shall be given a last chance to pass the exam.
Failure to pass, students shall be advised to take any Non - board programs of the
College,

Section C- Competency Exams
All incoming 2nd year, 3rd year and 4th year students enrolled in any board and
non-board programs are required to take and pass the Competency Exams. Only
major subjects are included in the competency exams. This is a requirement prior to
their enrollment. If someone fails, he/she must undergo an intervention program of the
college.

Section D- Book Review
All students shall recite one (1) book review related to his/her subject enrolled
in a semester except for Math, Science, PE and NSTP. This is one of the academic
programs of the college to promote reading, comprehension and critical thinking of
PGPC students. This will form part of the student graded recitation.

Section H - Absences and Tardiness
Absences
An absence from class shall be considered as such for whatever reason the
student might have for missing his class; i.e. there is no difference between excused
and unexcused absences.
When a student unavoidably absent himself/herself from class, he/she should
present to the instructor /professor on his/her next meeting an EXCUSE SLIP from the
Guidance Office noted by the OSA Head/Coordinator.

Absences may either be:
Excused Absence-the reason is justifiable and acceptable (with valid proof/document)
such as health problem, death in the family, and accidents; this will entitle the student to
make up for the lost activity such as quiz, report, experiment, and other graded activities.
However, it is up to the satisfaction of the instructor/professor to design and schedule
the makeup activity.
Unexcused Absence - the reason is unreasonable and unacceptable as determined
by the dean the student cannot make up for the missed activity neither are the instructors
or professor have the right to give make up activities.
Excessive Absences
Per semester, a student is allowed a maximum hour of absence equivalent to three (3)
times the number of units of the subject concerned.
If the students exceed the number of absences allowed, the school shall
automatically DROP the said student from the class.
When the total hours missed by the student due to absence (excused or unexcused)
exceed 20% of the class hours for the entire semesters, he/she shall be automatically
dropped from the subject and is therefore not allowed to continue attending classes.
It is important that classroom teacher check the attendance regularly. Likewise,
students are expected to constantly check with their instructors/professors the number
of their absences in class to avoid surprises. In similar manner, teachers should forewarn
students who are habitually absent and late in class.

Chapter IV
STUDENT LOAD

Section A - Regular Study Load
For undergraduate students, regular academic load depends on the program they
are enrolled in but not more than 30 units in one given semester. Students are advised
to consult their dean or program chair before deciding on what subjects to enroll and
how many to take. Regular load during summer classes is 6 units, unless explicitly
indicated in the Plan of Study, in which case, summer class is required. Only graduating
students shall be allowed to enroll maximum of 9 units, with the approval of the VPAA.
Section B- Overload and Underload
Under no circumstances shall a student take under load in a semester. If intending
to take under loaded units, the student must seek the approval of the dean by filling out
permit to take underload. Students with special needs, like health, may be allowed to
take under load. A medical certificate has to be attached to the form to be validated by
the College physician. Working students, on the other hand, may be allowed to take
under load on special cases, with prior approval of the dean and the VPAA. Under
loading for two (2) consecutive semesters may be grounds for academic disciplinary
action, as consistent under loading will affect the maximum residency of the student to
finish the program.
Overloading is strictly monitored in the College. With the approval of the program
chair and the VPAA, a student is allowed to take an overload of not more than six (6)
units in one given semester provided that he/she is not a graduating student.

Section D - What do "INC" AND "DRP" mean?
1. What does "INC" mean?
"INC" means the student is passing but has failed to take the final examination
or to submit certain academic requirements of the course. In either case, the student
is presumed to be of "passing" standing such that completionn of such missed work
will only mean passing grade.
Completing the "INC" must be done the following semester and only to the
same instructor or professor who gave the grade. If the said instructor is no longer
connected with the College, the Program Chair shall assign another faculty member
to attend to the student seeking completionn. However, once the completing period
has lapsed, a grade of "5" shall be given to the student.

2. What does DRP means?
A subject may be dropped one week after the preliminary period. After such
period dropping is not allowed. Should the student discontinue attending the class
without officially dropping the subject, a grade of "DROP" will be given to him due
to excessive unexcused absences. Unofficially dropped (UOD) is when the student
has not shown up in class since the start of the semester and did not file LOA or
has incurred excessive unexcused absences. Officially dropped (OD) is when the
student has followed the dropping procedure, paid the dropping fee, and submitted
the dropping forms to the respective (dean and the registrar).

CHAPTER XIV
STUDENT ORGANIZATION

Section A- Rules and Requirements on the Student Organization
Students at the College shall have the right to form an organization. This
organization shall embody their ideals, visions, inclinations and perspectives which
are in consonance with the existing rules and regulations of the College. The kinds
of organization that will be considered for recognition include academic groups
clubs, socio-civic groups, sports, arts and culture, safety, religious groups, student
publications and among others.

1. Requirements in Forming Student Organizations
The following steps shall be needed in order for an organization to be recognized
a) The organization shall consist of not less than 20 members who are officially
enrolled students of PGPC during the semesters
b) The organization shall have a constitution which is in accordance with the
existing rules and regulations of the College as stated in the Student Manual as
well as the Constitution of the College.
c) The organization shall have an organizational structure representing the sets of
officers with the defined functions of each office.
d) The organization shall write a letter addressed to the Head/Director/Coordinator
of the Student Affairs indicating their intention to be recognized.
e) The organization must have an adviser. This adviser must be a regular full-time
faculty member of PGPC.
f) All organization officers must be in good academic standing, i.e., have no failing
grades.
g) Shall submit constitution and by-laws and at least one (1) year plans and
programs.
h) Recognition of an organization is renewable every academic year upon
submission of important requirements

2. Rules and Regulations on Student Organization
Once recognition is granted, the student organization agrees:
a) To seek the general welfare of its members.
b) To provide equal opportunity and participation to all members
c) To prohibit the practice of discrimination against all potential members because
of race, religion, sex, color, age, nationality, or disability.
d) To exercise reasonable means of recruitment process without harm to the
mental, emotional, and physical being of the potential member.
e) To constantly seek the guidance of the organization adviser.
f) To abide by the rules and regulations of the College pertaining to the students.
g) Students are only allowed to join not more than 2 organizations with no grade
lower than 2.50, no grade of 5.0, no DRP, not having suspended to any violations
of the college. All organizations shall comply all college policies and procedures
h) All students' organization shall not be renewed unless the following
accomplishment report are not submitted:
a. FS report
b. Photos as proof of the organization projects and activities

d. Other documents/report as required by OSA/Adviser

CHAPTER XVII
POLICIES AND PROCEDURES OF THE OFFICE OF
NATIONAL SERVICE TRAINING PROGRAM

Pursuant to the provisions of the Republic Act No. 9163 otherwise known as the
National Service Training Program (NSTP) Act of 2001, these policies and procedures
will be adopted and implemented in the administration and operation of the NSTP in
lhe Padre Garcia Polytechnic College.

Article I
Policy Statement

Wection 1. It is the policy or mandate of the College to conduct the training under
NSTP to ensure compliance to R.A. 9163 also known as the NSTP Act of 2001.
Wectlon 2. The training program seeks to develop civic consciousness and defense
preparedness among the students while enhancing their total well-being as model and
agent of community development, institutionalize the NSTP structure, systems and
procedure in the operation of the program and to build a pool of qualified trainers and
volunteers among the students and faculty members with the program core values.
Wection 3. All tertiary education institutions are required to offer the program and thus,
sstudents are required to undergo this training program.
Article II
Scope and Coverage

Wection 4. The policy and procedure cover the management and operation of the
NSTP program and activities in the College with its three components namely; Civic
Welfare Training Service (CWTS), Literacy Training Service (LTS) and Reserve
Officers Training Corps (ROTC) units of the College. The NSTP office are responsible
in the implementation of these policies and procedures.

Article III
Definition of Terms

Nection 6. When used, the following terms shall mean as provided herein.
0.1. "National Service Training Program" (NSTP) is a program aimed at enhancing
civic consciousness and defense preparedness in the youth by developing the ethics
of service and patriotism while undergoing training in any of its three (3) program


---------------------
PAGE 54 
components such as ROTC, CWTS and LTS. Its various components are specially
designed to enhance the youth's active contribution to the general welfare. (Sec.3
para. a, R.A. 9163).
6.2. "Civic Welfare Training Service" or CWTS refers to programs or activities
contributory to the general welfare and the betterment of life for the members of the
community or the enhancement of its facilities, especially those devoted to improving
health, education, environment, entrepreneurship, safety, recreation, and morals of
the citizenry. (Sec.3, para. d, R.A. 9163).
6.3. "Reserve Officers' Training Corps" (ROTC) refers to the program component,
institutionalized under Section 38 and 39 of Republic Act No.7077, designed to
provide military training to tertiary level students in order to motivate, train, organize
and mobilize them for national defense preparedness. (Sec.3 (b), R.A. 9163).
6.4. "NSTP Coordinator/Head" is a duly qualified and experienced personnel assigned
by the school authority as Chief or Head of the National Service Training Program
Office of the concerned institution. The same has adequate training and experience
on the different components of NSTP offered by the institution. (CHED Memo Order
(CMO) April 25, 2002)
6.5. "NSTP Coordinator" is a duly qualified and experienced personnel assigned by
college authority as Coordinator for Civic Welfare Training Service/Literacy Training
Service component of NSTP. The same has adequate training and experience in civic
welfare activities and programs. (CMO April 25, 2002; CMO #5, S. 2003)
6.6. "Instructor" is a duly qualified and experienced person assigned to handle any of
the NSTP components offered by the institution.
6.7. "NSTP Student" is a tertiary level student officially enrolled in any of the components
of NSTP conducted by the institution.
6.8. "CWTS Student" is a tertiary level student officially enrolled particularly in Civic
Welfare Training Service component of NSTP conducted by an institution.
6.9. "LTS Student" is a tertiary level student officially enrolled in Literacy Training
Service component of NSTP conducted by an institution.
6.10. "ROTC Student" is a tertiary level student officially enrolled in NSTP ROTC
conducted by an institution.
6.11. "NSTP Office" is an office which administers and supervises the implementation
of the different NSTP components conducted by an institution.
6.12. "Institution" is a duly authorized establishment of learning that offers and
conducts the National Service Training Program as part of students' curriculum in strict
compliance with Republic Act No. 9163, other pertinent laws, rules and regulations.

Weetlon A-Library Services

A.1 READERS' SERVICES

. OPAC (Online Public Access Catalog) - it is a digital system that allows
library users to search, locate, and access information about the library's
collection through a computer or online interface.
. Circulation Services - one of the main services of the PGPC Library, to
circulate and allow access to different library resources for use in research
and other academic requirements.
. Research Assistance - all faculty members and students are welcome to
address their individual library requirements and problems with the Librarian
on duty.
. Current Awareness Services -this service provides awareness to the faculty
members and students about newly acquired items. Students and faculty
members are welcome to browse the collection. This is done to inform library
customers about new library book acquisitions.
. Information Literacy Services - this service includes library orientation
about its organization and services. New students receive library instruction/
orientation at the beginning of each school year.
. Service Facilities - the service facilities provide educational support for
students, instructors, and employees.
. Interlibrary Loan - it allows student or faculty member who intends to use the
library facilities and resources of other libraries that are not available at PGPC
library. The researcher is directed to the library of her or his preference.

A.2 TECHNICAL SERVICES

. Collection Development - the Collection Development identifies appropriate
scholarly resources, acquires or licenses the utilization of library materials,
and conducts collection assessments. Its primary activities include discussing
with vendors and publishers for books, periodicals, and databases; acquiring
and processing gift materials for libraries; evaluating the collections' strengths
for accreditation; identifying needed research resources to support PGPC's
diverse curricula; managing material ordering systems; and providing technical
support for electronic resources. It also maintains the bibliographic records of
library collection.
. Cataloguing and Classification - the main function of the Cataloguing
and Classification is to provide access to library materials by creating and
maintaining accurate records in the online catalog for the general collection of

types of information in the library, assigning call numbers before they are

. Materials Processing - this service handles the post-cataloguing process

book pockets, and date-due slips. It is also responsible for tagging and

CHAPTER XXI
INTERNET USAGE POLICY

Section A-Statement of Policy
Padre Garcia Polytechnic College is committed to provide internet access in
a professional work environment. To ensure everyone has the same right and respect,
the College has instituted this College wide internet usage policy.
This policy is intended to inform all the employees and students of the
College the expectations and acceptable behaviors when using the internet.
Anyone who uses the internet are expected and required to follow this policy.
The College will not tolerate unacceptable internet behavior.
This policy is not intended to impede the normal behavior among the
employees, students of the College.

1. Scope of this Policy
It applies to everyone using the internet in the College. It includes all employees,
directors, deans, teachers, and students.

2. Definition
Internet usage includes, but not limited to the followings:
· Email
. Accessing Web sites
. Accessing News Groups
. Chat
. Files sharing/upload/download
. Telnet

3. Monitor Internet Usage
The College can monitor all internet usages. All internet activities can be logged
for further review. This is to ensure that all internet users will adhere to our policy.

4. Activities can be blocked
The College can and will block certain internet activities that are deemed
unsuitable and/or unacceptable.



---------------------
PAGE 64 
5. College purpose of Internet
All internet usages are meant generally for academic research purposes only and
if necessary, official transactions sanctioned by the College that requires internet
activities.

6. Activities Not Permitted
The following activities are deemed unproductive by the College and therefore
Not Permitted:
. Use foul/ obscene/offensive language/material
. Harassing, insulting others
. Violation of laws (copyright and others)
. Accessing sex/pornography/offensive and other improper materials
. Hacking, damaging computers
. Social networking activities
. Misrepresenting yourself/facts or others
. Others as mandated by the College

7. Objectionable Material
Users shall not access/upload/download materials that are deemed objectionable
to other student or employees. These include, but not limited to jokes, harassments
or discrimination of a certain group of people based on:
. Sex, Race, National Origin, Ethnic, Age
. Physical Ability/Appearances
. Sexual Orientation
. Religion
. Political Affiliation
. Marital/family/Social Status
. Language or Dialect
. Disability
. Medical Status/Conditions
. Or any other action that is prohibited by law

8. Confidential/Sensitive Material
Users should not upload/save/send College's confidential and/or sensitive
material to the public or any locations that are considered not appropriate or
insecure.
9. Liability
All students and employees are expected to adhere to this internet usage policy.
Internet misuse can result in suspension or termination internet usage or any
disciplinary action. It may also be personal liability under civil or criminal law.

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Section 1 - Suspension of Classes
Classes are automatically suspended during storm signal 3 or 4 and
when other natural disasters strike. In such cases, students need not to wait for the


-----------------
PAGE 65 

suspension announcement from the College administrators. However, classes may be
Suspended by the College President during:
1. Municipal holidays
2. Official LGU sponsored activities that require PGPC participation
3. Special College-wide events
4. All holidays/no classes, students are not allowed to enter the campus without
parent consent and approved request letter.

Bection 2 - Official and Unofficial Student Activities
Student activities are only called "official" when included in the College
Calendar such activities qualify as any of the following:
1. College sponsored
2. Department sponsored
3. OSA sponsored
4. No off-campus activities shall held using the name of the college without the
approval of the College President

Classroom-based activities such as rehearsing for a classroom activity, working on
a group project, meeting for planning an activity, and the likes are not official student
activities and therefore, are not sanctioned by the College. Activities initiated by a
faculty member without knowledge and authorization of the College administration are
not classified as official student activities.
Consequentially, "unofficial student activities" are not within the control and
accountability of the College administration.

Section 3 - Posting of Announcements
Posting of any kind on school bulletin boards has to have permission
and signature from the Office of Student Affairs. After two weeks, the sponsoring
organization must take to initiate in removing the posted materials. Posting of College
organizations may simply seek the permission of the College Dean. The College
shall provide posting areas. Materials posted outside these areas will be removed/
confiscated without notice, and the same may be claimed from the OSA. Posting of
announcements/reminders on any walls inside and outside of the College building,
classrooms, laboratories, and others is strictly prohibited.
Department/student organization's social media account shall be managed by the
College Dean, OSA, Program Chairs and other authorized college officials and
employees only. All institutional events, from national, local, college shall be first
posted to the official college page and shared to other social media account.

Section 4 - Student Clearance
Clearance is an important document for students who are continuing/
graduating applying for a leave of absence (LOA), or seeking for honorable dismissal.
A student is cleared when all financial, property, and academic accountabilities
from the various units of the College have been settled. Only after the student has
completed the clearance form will s/he be given an approval for enrollment and other
purposes.

Recognized student organizations and officially enrolled students may be
allowed to use school facilities and certain equipment. School facilities and equipment

Generally, all invitations to join academic contests, cultural competition, and

Peer gimmicks or class outings loosely organized by the students among
themselves are not sanctioned by the College and are therefore beyond the control,
supervision, and accountability of the college administration. Presence of teacher/s in
such activities, whether intentional or not, does not at all make the activities official or
college sanctioned.
